Traction reads the careers page of every venture-backed company it can find, every morning. That produces counts nobody else publishes. Here is what today's reading says about 49,800 open roles at 4,443 funded companies.

The daily series

Traction has recorded a reading every morning since 2026-09-22 — 44 of them. No other public source on startup hiring publishes daily: Ashby reports once a year on 1,200 startups, Carta on about 3,000, and the rest are point-in-time samples.

The line below is not a hiring index, and reading it as one would be wrong. Between September and November the total went from 16,854 to 49,800 roles — that is mostly Traction adding companies, not the market tripling. Coverage and market have to be separated, so they are.

The panel starts today. 4,443 companies were fixed on 2026-11-15 and will be followed at constant membership from here, whether they hire or not. The first comparable reading lands tomorrow, and the series becomes meaningful over months, not days. It cannot be backfilled: the logs only kept daily totals, not per-company counts.

Hiring is concentrated

Of 4,443 companies with something open, 47% of all roles sit with the top 10% (the top 1% hire 10% · the top 5% hire 30% · the top 10% hire 47% · the top 25% hire 72%). The median company has 5 roles open, and 708 of them — 16% — are hiring for exactly one person. A startup job market is mostly a long tail of companies making a single hire.

How this is counted

Every number on this page is a count of job posts read directly from each company's own careers page, every morning. Nothing is modelled, estimated, weighted or projected. A role that comes down disappears the next day.

There are no trends here, on purpose. Traction has only been observing since 25 September 2026, and the publication dates on older posts suffer from survivorship bias — a role posted in April and still open is a role nobody took. Claiming a hiring trend from that would be wrong, so these are dated snapshots instead.

The companies come from the published portfolios of venture funds, and only those whose system exposes a public job feed can be read. This describes venture-backed hiring, not the labour market.
